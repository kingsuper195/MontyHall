The Monty Hall Paradox

The Monty Hall Paradox is thus: At a game show, the host presents a player three doors. Behind two randomly assigned doors are goats, and behind the other one is a shiny new car. The player chooses one, and the host opens one of the other two doors. Behind this door is a goat. The host offers that the player can choose to switch doors from their original door to the other, unrevealed door.

Is it in the player's advantage to switch?

The obvious thought would be no, as the probality of getting the car would remain 1/3, right? It turns out the answer is yes. The player's intial choice is, of course 1/3, however if they are to change, after the host reveals the other door, their chance becomes 2/3. Why? No one really knows, though there have been explainations offered.

This Tool

In automatic mode, this tool simulates the paradox, over and over again, until the law of large numbers presents itself, and you can see it proved.

In manual mode, it allows you to play through the scenario, as many times as you want, and see it in action yourself.

Of course, one could always argue that it is never in the player's favour to switch, using this reasoning.
